\section{What the checker currently does}

The checker verifies \emph{detailed balance} (DB) for every pair of states
$(s_i, s_j)$ in a connected component:
\[
  T(s_i \to s_j)\,\pi(s_i) \;=\; T(s_j \to s_i)\,\pi(s_j),
  \qquad \pi(s) \propto e^{-\beta E(s)}.
\]
For a component with $N$ states this requires evaluating $\binom{N}{2} =
N(N-1)/2$ symbolic expressions.  For the VMMC \texttt{vmmc\_lattice.wl}
example at NGrid\,=\,3 this is roughly $504 \times 503/2 \approx 126{,}000$
pairs (most trivially zero due to sparse connectivity, but the symbolic
work is dominated by the $\sim$thousands of non-trivial ones).  The current
runtime is around three minutes.

\section{The symmetry group of the algorithm}

\begin{definition}[G-invariance of the transition kernel]
  Let $G$ be a group acting on the state space $\mathcal{S}$.  The transition
  kernel $T$ is \emph{$G$-invariant} if
  \[
    T(g \cdot s \to g \cdot s') \;=\; T(s \to s')
    \quad \forall\, g \in G,\; s, s' \in \mathcal{S}.
  \]
  The energy is $G$-invariant if $E(g \cdot s) = E(s)$ for all $g, s$,
  which implies $\pi(g \cdot s) = \pi(s)$.
\end{definition}

For algorithms on a periodic $L \times L$ square lattice, two natural
symmetry groups arise.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Translations.}
  $G = \mathbb{Z}_L \times \mathbb{Z}_L$, $|G| = L^2$.  The group element
  $(a,b)$ shifts every site $(r,c) \mapsto (r+a, c+b) \bmod L$.  A VMMC
  or Kawasaki algorithm is translation-invariant whenever the energy depends
  only on pairwise distances (not absolute positions) and the proposal
  distribution is spatially uniform.  Both conditions hold for
  \texttt{vmmc\_lattice.wl}.

\item \textbf{Full square symmetry (D4 $\times$ translations).}
  If the algorithm is also invariant under the 8 rotations and reflections
  of the square, $|G| = 8L^2$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
For concreteness we focus on translations ($|G| = L^2$); every argument
applies unchanged to larger groups.

\section{Why G-invariance reduces the number of pairs to check}

\begin{theorem}[Pair-orbit reduction]
  Suppose $T$ and $E$ are both $G$-invariant and $G$ acts freely on
  $\mathcal{S}$ (no non-identity element fixes any state).  Then DB holds
  for all $N(N-1)/2$ pairs if and only if it holds for a set of
  representative pairs — one from each $G$-orbit of unordered pairs.
  The number of such orbits is
  \[
    \frac{N(N-1)}{2\,|G|}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The $G$-orbit of an unordered pair $\{s, s'\}$ is the set
  $\{\{g \cdot s,\; g \cdot s'\} : g \in G\}$.
  Under a free action, all $|G|$ images are distinct, so each orbit has
  exactly $|G|$ elements.  Since every pair belongs to exactly one orbit,
  the total number of orbits is $\binom{N}{2}/|G| = N(N-1)/(2|G|)$.

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  \emph{Sufficiency.}
  Suppose DB holds for a representative pair $\{s_1, s_2\}$.
  For any $g \in G$:
  \begin{align*}
    T(g \cdot s_1 \to g \cdot s_2)\,\pi(g \cdot s_1)
    &= T(s_1 \to s_2)\,\pi(s_1) \\
    &= T(s_2 \to s_1)\,\pi(s_2) \\
    &= T(g \cdot s_2 \to g \cdot s_1)\,\pi(g \cdot s_2),
  \end{align*}
  where the outer equalities use $G$-invariance of $T$ and $\pi$.
  Hence DB holds for every pair in the orbit.

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  \emph{Necessity.}
  DB for the orbit member $(g \cdot s_1, g \cdot s_2)$ immediately implies
  DB for $(s_1, s_2)$ by the same argument with $g$ replaced by $g^{-1}$.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Speedup factor]
  With $G$-invariance the checker needs to evaluate exactly $\mathbf{1/|G|}$
  as many symbolic DB expressions.  The speedup is $|G|$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection*{With full D4 + translations ($|G| = 72$)}
\vspace{-0.3em}
For a $3\times 3$ torus the full symmetry group (8 rotations/reflections
$\times$ 9 translations) has order 72.  The same $504$-state component
would require only $126{,}756/72 \approx 1{,}760$ pair checks and an
estimated runtime of $\sim 2{-}3$ seconds.

\medskip
For NGrid\,=\,6 ($N \approx 100{,}000$ states) the untreated checker would
require $\sim 5 \times 10^9$ pairs — computationally infeasible — whereas
with $|G| = 36$ (translations) this reduces to $\sim 1.4 \times 10^8$
pairs, and with $|G| = 288$ (D4 + translations) to $\sim 1.7 \times 10^7$.

\section{The user's concern: what about transitions \emph{within} an orbit?}

The key objection is: two states in the \emph{same} G-orbit can transition
to each other (e.g.\ a particle cluster translating to a
symmetry-equivalent configuration).  Do we not need to check those pairs
independently?

\smallskip
\noindent
The answer is that such pairs are \emph{included} in the orbit-reduction
count — they are not a special exception.

\medskip
Consider two states in the same orbit: $s_1 = c$ (the canonical
representative) and $s_2 = h \cdot c$ for some $h \neq e$.  This is still
a valid unordered pair $\{c, h \cdot c\}$, and its $G$-orbit is
$\{\{g \cdot c,\; g h \cdot c\} : g \in G\}$, which has $|G|$ elements
(under a free action).  DB for this pair reads
\[
  T(c \to h \cdot c)\,\pi(c) = T(h \cdot c \to c)\,\pi(h \cdot c).
\]
By $G$-invariance of $\pi$ this simplifies to
\begin{equation}
  T(c \to h \cdot c) \;=\; T(h \cdot c \to c) \;=\; T(c \to h^{-1} \cdot c),
  \label{eq:intra}
\end{equation}
where the second equality uses $G$-invariance of $T$ (apply $h^{-1}$ to both
states).  Equation~\eqref{eq:intra} is a \emph{proposal-symmetry} condition:
the probability of moving the cluster by group element $h$ must equal the
probability of the inverse move $h^{-1}$.

For any algorithm whose displacement proposal satisfies $P(\mathbf{d}) =
P(-\mathbf{d})$ — which is true by construction for \texttt{vmmc\_lattice.wl}
(the uniform-box list contains $\mathbf{d}$ and $-\mathbf{d}$ as a pair) —
equation~\eqref{eq:intra} holds automatically.  These intra-orbit DB
conditions therefore require \emph{no additional checking}: they are
guaranteed by the same proposal-symmetry argument that underlies the VMMC
detailed-balance proof.

Even without that argument: intra-orbit pairs number at most
$M \cdot |G|(|G|-1)/2 \approx N|G|/2 = O(N)$, whereas inter-orbit pairs
number $M(M-1)|G|^2/2 \approx N^2/(2|G|) = O(N^2)$.  For large $N$ the
intra-orbit contribution is negligible, and the dominant $O(N^2)$ cost still
shrinks by exactly $|G|$.

\section{What implementation is needed}

Three components are required to realise the speedup in practice.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Verify $G$-invariance.}  For each canonical seed $c$, compare
  the BFS output (transition probabilities from $c$) with the BFS output
  from a non-canonical orbit member $g \cdot c$.  G-invariance holds iff the
  two outputs agree after applying $g^{-1}$ to all successor states.  This
  is an exact symbolic check with negligible overhead.

\item \textbf{Canonicalise states.}  Map each state to the lexicographically
  smallest member of its G-orbit before adding it to the ``already seen''
  set.  Only one BFS is run per orbit; BFS from $g \cdot c$ is replaced by
  looking up the result for $c$ and applying $g$.

\item \textbf{Check one pair per G-orbit.}  For each pair of canonical
  representatives $(c_i, c_j)$, check only the pair $(c_i, c_j)$ itself
  (or any single orbit representative).  The $|G|-1$ other pairs in the
  same orbit are certified by Theorem 1 without additional computation.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
None of these steps changes the \emph{algebraic} content of the DB check;
they reduce the \emph{number of times} it is performed.
